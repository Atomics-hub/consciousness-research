# Paper 10 G0 Report — Finite Intervention-Basis Certificate

**Author and accountable operator:** Thomas Ryan  
**AI role:** research assistance only; not an author  
**Decision date:** 27 August 2026  
**Outcome:** NO-GO as a positive theorem program

## Decision

The proposed finite intervention-basis certificate fails its own novelty gate.
For stable linear systems, finite Markov-parameter tests, minimal realization,
Hankel methods, and robust error bounds already provide the relevant positive
results. For piecewise-linear systems with white-box access, exact or relaxed
verification is already formulated through reachability, SMT/MILP, simulation
relations, and abstraction. With black-box access alone, the residual guarantee
reduces to classical optimal recovery or global optimization under explicit
regularity and coverage assumptions.

Calling these inherited results an “intervention-basis theorem” would relabel
prior art. The positive program is therefore terminated before any new FlyVis
candidate outcome is opened.

## Strongest surviving seam

Current brain-emulation and behavioral-reconstruction work increasingly
recommends functional testing under novel perturbations. That is stronger than
ordinary held-out prediction, but it still does not distinguish:

1. a finite empirical battery that can find a counterexample;
2. a distributional claim over an explicit perturbation law; and
3. a uniform certificate over a continuous intervention domain.

The third claim requires additional structural or regularity-and-coverage
assumptions. This distinction is not a new theorem of control or approximation
theory. Its possible contribution is an operational claim-audit framework for
neural replacement and brain-emulation fidelity, where the distinction is
currently consequential and often left implicit.

## Prior-art pressure

- Linear realization and finite Markov-parameter equivalence make the linear
  positive case classical.
- Approximate simulation/bisimulation and symbolic abstraction already give
  assumption-bounded dynamical guarantees.
- Neural-network verification already separates testing from sound universal
  verification; exact verification is computationally hard for broad ReLU
  classes.
- Lipschitz global optimization and information-based complexity already imply
  covering-number lower bounds for black-box uniform guarantees.
- Functional-test proposals for whole-brain emulation already emphasize broad,
  multidimensional batteries, while recent behavioral-reconstruction work
  explicitly advocates held-out perturbations.

## Pivot rule

Only the claim-boundary synthesis advances. It must:

- credit every mathematical component as inherited;
- supply exact finite-battery counterexamples and fail-closed diagnostics;
- make its practical novelty the separation of falsification, distributional
  validation, and worst-case certification claims;
- include candidate-inclusive reachability and interface scope; and
- retain a claim ceiling that excludes consciousness, identity, survival,
  biological replaceability, and substrate independence.
